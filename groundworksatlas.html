Problem 1 · fixed

Systems are domain-scoped, and water quality is signed

The five systems broke at the waterline: an oyster's real service is nutrient removal, and scoring that as Soil 5 is biologically nonsense — while kelp performs neither pollination nor terrestrial pest control. The repair is not to bolt a sixth number onto all fifty buildings. It is to scope each system to a domain and let entities be graded only on the systems they can physically act in.

Universal · scored everywhere

  • 01FoodEdible or saleable output entering the market chain.
  • 02Biogas & Organic RecoveryDigestible or compostable residue, graded on collectability — not mass. Slurry beats pasture.

Land domain only

  • 03SoilNitrogen, organic matter, structure, cover. Applied to the following crop, not the farm.
  • 04PollinationFlowering resource and managed colony support.
  • 05Pest & Weed PressureCompetition, allelopathy, beneficial-insect habitat.

Water domain only

  • 06Water Quality & Nutrient RecoveryDissolved nitrogen and particulate load added to, or filtered out of, a coastal lease.

Inapplicable is not zero

An entity shows — on systems outside its domain and is never penalised for them. Ranking uses share of applicable maximum, so a four-system land crop and a three-system oyster lease compete on the same footing.

Water quality runs −3 to +5

It is the only signed system. Fed species load the water; extractive species strip it. A lease's net is the sum of everything inside it, so a salmon pen alone sits negative and has to be paid for.

Closed gap: Pest & weed was one entity deep

Cereal rye was the only entity scoring 4+, against four to seven in every other system — a system the player solves once and never revisits. Fixed by promoting buckwheat and forage radish to 4 on the weed-suppression evidence already gathered, and adding brown mustard as a biofumigant brassica at 5. Rye now has rivals with different second jobs, so the choice is a real rotation decision rather than a scripted one.

Amphibious buildings score in both

The Aquaponics Greenhouse, Compost Facility, Biogas Plant and Market Hall touch land and water chains, so they carry values on both sides. Nothing else does — this is a small set, deliberately.

Design layer

The throughput model

Food stops being a sixth stat sitting beside the others. It becomes the output, and the five service systems become the terms that multiply it — so ignoring soil, pollination, pest, energy or water quality costs real money instead of nothing. None of the constants below come from the research; they are the design layer, tuned to make a good rotation worth roughly twice a bad one.

yield = base_yield × soil_mult × (1 − pest_loss)
revenue = yield × base_price × quality_mult
net = revenue − (energy_cost × energy_mult) − labour
slots = floor(base_slots × (1 + 0.15 × net_water)) // marine leases

Fields carry state, crops do not

Every field holds soil and pest, each clamped to −3 … +5. At harvest the crop applies delta = score × 0.4. At the start of each season both values drift 10% back toward zero. This is what makes the effect land on the next crop rather than acting as a farm-wide buff.

Repeating a family bites

Plant the same crop family twice in a row and the field takes an extra −1.5 pest, while any positive soil delta is halved. Stacking, so a third repeat hurts more. This is the rule that makes mustard-after-cabbage a mistake the game can actually express.

Soil and pest are signed now

Corn and potato sit at −2 soil, cabbage at −2 pest, and overstocked pasture flips sheep negative. Without a negative half you cannot build a bad farm, only a less-good one — and diversification wins by default instead of by beating something.

Design layer

Knowledge & discovery

The model is deep on purpose, but a player handed six systems and two hidden field variables on day one will read the result as randomness rather than skill. So knowledge is earned per entity: plant a thing to see what happened, study it to learn why. Complexity arrives at the pace the player buys it, and the research buildings that had no economic role finally get one.

Hide the explanation, never the consequence

Observed always shows what happened. Gate the why, never the what — a player who cannot see that yield fell has been handed the randomness problem this system exists to solve.

Never gate the escape route

A player who has wrecked a field and cannot afford the laboratory must still be able to recover. Tired ↓ is enough to know to change something, even without knowing what clover does. Research buys precision, not survival.

Design layer

Costing & unlock order

Every entity priced off one spine: a season is 28 days, a year is four seasons. Only the base figures are authored — cost, cycle length, yield, unit price and running cost. Prices then follow a progression curve rather than a flat scale: the multiplier is 20 × 2.1^step, so each step costs about twice the last and the economy spans 900 to 51M across ten steps. Owning one of everything costs 320,021,300, a completed farm earns roughly 147,477,910 a year, and reaching a billion takes about five further years of full output — a millionaire well before the endgame, a billionaire only long past it. Cost and revenue take the same multiplier, so margin and payback are identical to the unscaled model. Margin, per-day return and payback are all derived, so the sheet cannot drift out of agreement with itself. Seed, spat and weaners are treated as cost of goods and charged every cycle; buildings, leases, orchards and breeding stock are capital and amortise across five years.

Unlocks are derived, not authored

There is no separate tech tree to maintain. A building opens one step after the earliest thing that can feed it — the Creamery because you have a Dairy Barn making milk, the Cider House because you have apples. 75 entities are gated this way, permits included — and because the permits are themselves chain-gated, the gates cannot be bought around. Twenty-five have nothing upstream at all (utilities, the nursery, the leases) and are gated by chapter and cost instead, and the twenty-one organisms open when you can buy seed or stock.

Not everything has a prerequisite

Exponential rewards cannot be gated by price. With each step earning several times the last, saving up stops nobody — fifteen entities at step 3 or later had no prerequisite at all, including a crop worth 130× the opening plot. Five permits close it: Field Deed, Livestock Licence, Orchard Deed, Utility Permit and Coastal Lease Permit, each issued by buildings the farm already operates, so the gates cannot be bought around. Nothing at step 3 or later is skippable now, and the 14 still ungated are the opening plots and services — the tree prints their price instead of a parent line.

107 edges, not 223

Most flows keep an existing building busy rather than opening a new one. Filtering to just the prerequisites cuts the graph by 52%, and the result is verified acyclic: no entity is ever its own ancestor. 75 entities are opened by a chain and only 12 by cost alone. Ten steps, deepened by the permits themselves. Every valid route is drawn, not merely the earliest — the Biogas Plant opens on kitchen waste, dairy slurry or pig slurry, and a tree that showed only the first would be lying about the design.

Where it stops being a tree

54 entities have exactly one prerequisite — those parts really are a tree. The other 21 converge: Grain Silos opens on any of wheat, barley, corn or field pea; the Biogas Plant on kitchen waste, dairy slurry or pig slurry; the Market Hall on the packhouse, cellar or coop. Service edges are deliberately not gates: the Field Laboratory samples wheat and the vet visits the herd, which are services about a thing rather than requirements for it. Counting them once put wheat at step 7, behind the Utility Permit. Those convergences are the whole reason two players reach the same building by different routes.

One input opens it — except its namesake

A building opens on any single input and gains its other recipes later; requiring all of them would gate the Bakery behind the entire farm. The exception is a processor named for its input. Six are: the Bakery needs flour, the Creamery milk, the Cider House apples, the Butchery the loading yard, the Flour Mill wheat, the Aquaculture Processing Plant the packing house. Without that rule the Bakery opened on eggs alone, with no flour anywhere on the farm.

Red clover is the only entity that loses money

Deliberately. It costs 1,740 in seed and returns about 1,160, a loss of roughly 928 a cycle. It earns its place through the next crop: clover into rye into corn lifts that corn from 80 units to 121, worth about +7,146. A 7× return, paid one season late. If any crop should teach the rotation system, it is the one with a red number on it.

Processors need feeding

A field yields about 0.75 units a day averaged across the roster, so the Fields column says how much farm a building actually needs. Build the Flour Mill too early and it idles — that constraint, not the build cost, is what should gate the mid game.
